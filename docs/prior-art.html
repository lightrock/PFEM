PFEM Prior Art and Positioning

PFEM means Polycentric Federated Evidence Mesh.

This page is a plain-language prior-art map for PFEM: a reference architecture for evidence governance, provenance, custody, confidence, evidence packages, rollups, and AI/human repository discipline across polycentric systems.

No novelty overclaim: provenance modeling, chain-of-custody practice, digital forensics, threat-intelligence exchange, government information exchange models, and audit-oriented evidence handling are established areas. PFEM should not claim that structured evidence, provenance, or inter-organizational sharing are new.

What Is Already Real Prior Art

Web provenance standards

W3C PROV defines a family of documents for interoperable provenance interchange in heterogeneous environments. It is direct prior art for representing entities, activities, agents, and derivation relationships.

PFCOMM as internal sibling reference architecture

PFCOMM, Polycentric Federated Command Mesh, is this project family's command/action coordination sibling. It is internal prior art for keeping authority, intent, tasking, assignments, action receipts, operational status, decisions, and after-action records separate from evidence governance.

PFEM should treat PFCOMM as the adjacent command/action substrate. PFEM should preserve claims, evidence, custody, confidence, provenance, evidence packages, and rollups without trying to become the command authority or tasking system.

What PFEM Should Not Claim

  • Do not claim invention of provenance modeling.
  • Do not claim invention of chain of custody or digital evidence handling.
  • Do not claim invention of inter-organizational information exchange.
  • Do not claim invention of cyber threat intelligence sharing.
  • Do not claim that dashboards, reports, or rollups are source evidence.
  • Do not claim PFEM is a command-and-control system.

Potentially Defensible Contribution

PFEM's defensible contribution is a contract-heavy reference architecture for polycentric federated evidence governance: keeping raw evidence, observations, findings, alerts, evidence packages, rollups, reports, provenance, custody, confidence, and sharing boundaries explicit.

The stronger research angle is not "evidence has provenance." It is: how heterogeneous human, AI, sensor, infrastructure, field, and institutional nodes expose auditable evidence surfaces that can federate without collapsing into one central truth source or one vendor-shaped dashboard.

Evidence-Governance Framing

PFEM should be presented as an evidence-governance substrate, not as a complete operational platform.

  • Raw evidence: original source material remains distinct from normalized observations.
  • Observations and findings: interpreted records remain separate from source evidence.
  • Provenance: origin, handling, derivation, timing, and attribution remain inspectable.
  • Custody: evidence handling and transfer are explicit rather than implied.
  • Confidence: claims carry uncertainty or confidence metadata rather than pretending to be final truth.
  • Packages and rollups: shareable summaries do not erase the underlying boundary between evidence and interpretation.
  • Receipts and checks: repository work and generated boundaries leave auditable traces.

Relationship to PFCOMM

PFEM and PFCOMM should remain distinct. PFEM governs claims, evidence, provenance, custody, confidence, evidence packages, and rollups. PFCOMM governs authority context, intent, tasking, assignments, action receipts, operational status, decisions, after-action records, and command rollups.

The same future contract body may expose both surfaces for one participant, but PFEM should not merge evidence truth and command authority into one ambiguous object.

DARPA-Proposal Posture

A credible proposal should position PFEM as a synthesis over known evidence, provenance, forensics, and information-sharing prior art:

This work does not propose another evidence database, dashboard, or provenance ontology. It proposes a contract-heavy reference architecture for accountable evidence participants, separating evidence governance from command/action coordination while allowing heterogeneous nodes to exchange attributable claims, evidence packages, confidence metadata, custody receipts, and rollups across policy-bounded coalitions.

Open Questions

  • What is the minimal evidence contract that covers human reports, sensors, AI agents, infrastructure nodes, field laptops, and institutional systems?
  • Which evidence obligations belong in PFEM, which action obligations belong in PFCOMM, and which belong in a third shared contract-body project?
  • How should PFEM represent AI-generated claims, model outputs, tool logs, and agent-created scripts without treating them as trusted truth?
  • How should evidence packages preserve provenance and custody across disconnected or degraded nodes?
  • How do many PFEM nodes federate without erasing local autonomy or creating one false central truth source?
